With Clive in India

In the year 1745, young Robert Clive departed from Portsmouth aboard the merchant vessel Winchester, bound for the distant shores of Madras. The journey would take many fortnights, sailing around the Cape of Good Hope, through waters infested with French privateers.

At that time, the British East India Company maintained only a handful of trading posts along the Coromandel Coast. The great Mughal Empire, though weakened, still held nominal sovereignty over much of the subcontinent. Few could have imagined that within two decades, a clerk from Market Drayton would reshape the destiny of India.

Clive was but eighteen years old, melancholic and seemingly ill-suited for commerce. His father had secured him a writership at Madras, hoping the position might provide the wayward youth with direction and fortune. Little did anyone suspect that this troubled young man possessed the martial genius that would make him master of Bengal.
